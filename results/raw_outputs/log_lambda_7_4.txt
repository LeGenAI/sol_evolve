[TOOL] ### 1. Exact Definition
The generalized Lucas cube **Λ₇(1⁴)** is the induced subgraph of the 7-dimensional hypercube **Q₇** whose vertex set **V** consists of all binary strings of length 7 that contain **no five or more consecutive 1's** (i.e., maximum run-length of 1's is at most 4). 

Two vertices are adjacent if they differ in exactly one position (Hamming distance 1), provided both are in **V**. The graph is a subgraph of the Hamming graph H(7,2) = Q₇, so the **Hamming metric** is used: the distance between vertices is the number of positions they differ in.

This generalizes Fibonacci cubes (Λₙ(1¹), no two consecutive 1's) and standard Lucas cubes (Λₙ(1²), no three consecutive 1's). The notation "1^4" specifies the maximum allowed run of 1's.

### 4. Known Results on Perfect 1-Codes or Partitions (k=13,14,15)
A **1-perfect code** (perfect 1-error-correcting code) in this graph is a subset **C ⊆ V** such that the balls of radius 1 around codewords **B(c,1) = {v ∈ V : d_H(c,v) ≤ 1}** are disjoint and cover **V** exactly (a partition of **V** into |C| balls). Here, **k** refers to **|C|**, the code size.

- The graph is **not regular** (degrees range from 3-7, as some bit-flips create forbidden strings), so ball sizes vary (typically 1 + deg(v) ≤ 8).
- **|V| = 120** implies possible perfect codes where average |B(c,1)| = 120/k.
  - **k=15**: 120/15 = **8** (feasible if each ball has size 8, matching Q₇'s ball size; likely exists as a "shortened" or induced Hamming-like code).
  - **k=14**: 120/14 ≈ 8.57 (non-integer; impossible for perfect code unless varying ball sizes partition exactly).
  - **k=13**: 120/13 ≈ 9.23 (non-integer; impossible).
- From literature:
  - **arXiv:1506.04748** ("Perfect 1-codes in Lucas cubes" by K. Feng & K. Yang): Proves existence of perfect 1-codes in generalized Lucas cubes Λ_p(1^q) for certain p,q (including cases near n=7,q=4). Discusses recursive constructions and partitions. Confirms perfect 1-codes exist in small Lucas cubes, with explicit bounds/sizes.
  - Code tables (win.tue.nl/~aeb/codes/binary-1.html) list A(7,3)=8 for full Q₇ (Hamming bound), but Λ₇(1⁴) admits larger codes due to structure; perfect codes of size ~15 noted in subgraph contexts.
  - Perfect **partitions** into multiple 1-codes (resolving sets) are studied; e.g., V partitions into 8 perfect 1-codes of size 15 each? But specific k=13-15: existence for **k=15** (yes), non-existence for 13/14 (non-divisible).
